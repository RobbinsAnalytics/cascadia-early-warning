Report counts are not incident rates or measures of device safety. This independent public-data demonstration provides no medical, legal or regulatory advice.

  • Counts, not rates. In FDA's own words, from the openFDA documentation: MDR data alone cannot be used to establish rates of events; a causal relationship cannot be established between product and the reactions listed in a report; and every API response says to assume all results are unvalidated. Reporting is passive and batched, and a count carries no denominator. A quiet code is not a safe code.
  • Summary reports. NPT is on FDA's list of product codes eligible for voluntary malfunction summary reporting, and DSP, DSQ, LWS, NIK, OZD and PYX are not. 4,179 of NPT's 72,845 reports are summary reports standing for 149,569 events, so its count can move with reporting practice and is not a count of events.
  • The snapshot. Receipts run through 2026-08-31, as the source had loaded them on 2026-09-29. This is a retrospective test using the October 2026 data snapshot. It does not reconstruct exactly what was publicly available at each historical date.
  • Reports without an event date. 13,375 of the headline code's eligible reports carry none; they are counted in charts 1 to 4 and left out of chart 5 only.
  • What public data cannot supply. Exposure by lot and site, verification and validation results, the defect backlog, supplier and process measures, and complaint intake before it becomes a report.
  • What this page refuses to do. It names no manufacturer or brand, publishes no rate and no risk, and does not read the recall timeline as a validation of the rule.

05 / Method and receipts

How each figure was made, and what checked it

The decision

The decision this page serves is a quality team's monthly choice of where to look first: which product codes are reporting as expected and which are not.

Whose decision
the owner of a post-market review queue
Horizon
Outlook: November 2026, three months from the August data origin.Review rule: one-month-ahead forecasts.
Literacy
a reader who works in spreadsheets and reads a trend line, not a statistician
Benchmark
the trailing three-month mean, the figure a spreadsheet would already give
Cadence
monthly as the source loads
Action
open a review or do not

The review rule

  • Registered before the first forecast existed (governance/pre-registration.md, commit fbf0f41), under the model in use at one month ahead.
  • The rule. A month is flagged when the observed count exceeds the 80% upper bound and is at least five reports above the point forecast. Two consecutive flagged months open an episode. One run is one episode, and an empty queue is a valid result.
  • One-sided. The rule is one-sided by design: a month below its range is never flagged.
  • The floor. The five-report floor is a workload heuristic, chosen so that a departure of a handful of reports in a small series never opens an episode; it is not a calibrated threshold.
  • By chance. An illustration, not a measured false-alarm rate: if a model's 80% ranges were correctly calibrated and its errors independent from month to month, a month would land above the upper bound about one time in ten and two consecutive months about one time in a hundred, so about one episode per hundred evaluated months would open by chance before the five-report floor. Neither condition is guaranteed here, and no false-alarm rate was measured.

When the rule is on (the coverage gate)

  • The gate. Each code's rule was enabled from its 80% coverage in the 2024-01 to 2025-12 locked test: the first 24 of the 32 months, 2024-01 to 2026-08, that the rule then runs over.
  • With the gate the rule ran on four codes, 128 code-months, and opened 1 episode.
  • Without it the same rule, over all 224 code-months, opens 7 (DSP 1, OZD 2 and PYX 4) and flags 30 months.
  • Against chance, as the pre-registration says the workload is read: at about one episode per hundred evaluated months, chance alone would open about 1.3 in the 128 code-months with the rule on and about 2.2 in all 224; the rule opened one and seven. The illustration's conditions do not hold for the codes whose ranges failed the gate.
  • Reading coverage. An 80% range should hold about four months in five; across the seven codes the model in use's ranges held between 33% and 100% of locked-test months, and ranges too wide and too narrow are both calibration failures. A code with the rule off is not reviewed by this module.

Counts, forecast arithmetic, scores and review episodes were re-derived down a second path

  • The freeze. 649,080 distinct reports across the seven codes (649,083 report-code memberships: three reports carry more than one of the codes), pulled from openFDA in 696 pages whose SHA-256 is recorded in the manifest and listed in the source register. The monthly counts reconcile with zero variance, on every one of 896 code-months, against the source's own daily count series pulled separately.
  • The exclusion. A private token list, kept outside the repository, removed 1 report whole; its SHA-256 (5fa19c0913152ef4...) and the counts removed per field are published, and no firm is named anywhere.
  • Two paths. A separately written path, DuckDB SQL over the staged FDA pages plus its own Python arithmetic, recomputes the monthly and lag-matched counts, the distinct report totals, every forecast point and range, the scores, the review episodes, and the queue with and without its coverage gate as this page publishes it, and they must agree before anything is published. It does not refit the ETS model: candidate points are recomputed from the model's exported states, and ranges and scores from the published points. The recall timeline is checked only against fourteen independently verified Class I events, and the cohort gate, the exclusion receipt, the reports without an event date, the promotion decision, the pre-registered recall count, the summary-report composition, the remedial-recall counts and the outlook's twenty dots are carried from the build without a second derivation. The second path is validate_measures.py.
  • Golden fixtures, computed outside both engines and committed before any engine code: both baselines on a made series, the ETS recurrence from fixed states, the review rule with a reset, the lag-matched count and the interval scores, passing on both paths.
  • Proof the checks can fail. The validation report re-runs the 21 checks of the domain gate against deliberately corrupted copies of their input in 46 scenarios, from a missing partition and a moved hash to a page that states the wrong device class, and records that every one tripped. Every check has at least one scenario. The second path has no such proof mode of its own.
  • Chronology. The git log shows the pre-registration and the frozen harness before the first forecast row and the promotion decision before the first locked row. One corrected result was frozen after a documented harness repair; promotion and point forecasts were unchanged.
  • The chart review and two reading panels of simulated AI reviewer seats, under VIZ-PRINCIPLES v2.8 and CHART-REVIEW v2.8, recorded in the chart review.
