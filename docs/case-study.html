01 / The review decision

Which product codes deserve a closer look this month

The decision is a quality team's monthly choice of where to look first: which product codes are reporting as expected and which are not. This build's contribution is testing report-volume forecasts and a repeatable review rule on public data: a forecast of each code's reports with a stated range, a held-out test of whether ranges like it held, and a fixed rule that turns departures into a review queue.

  • The cohort. Seven cardiovascular product codes spanning Classes II and III, read from openFDA's device-event endpoint: 649,080 distinct reports, 649,079 after a private firm-list exclusion removed 1, counted per code per receipt month over 896 code-months (a report filed under two codes counts once in each).
  • Summary reports. NPT is on FDA's list of product codes eligible for voluntary malfunction summary reporting, and DSP, DSQ, LWS, NIK, OZD and PYX are not. 4,179 of NPT's 72,845 reports are summary reports standing for 149,569 events, so its count can move with reporting practice and is not a count of events.
  • Counts, not rates. Report counts are not incidence rates, event rates or measures of device safety, as FDA's own documentation says and the module page quotes. No manufacturer or brand is named, by decision.

02 / The measured results

What the test found

  • The outlook. For DSQ (Ventricular (Assist) Bypass), about 736 reports in November 2026, three months ahead, with an 80% range of 376 to 906; at that horizon ranges like it held 20 of 22 locked-test months. September 2026, one month ahead, is an elapsed-period estimate and is not yet in the source.
  • The test. Rolling forecasts were fitted only through each origin and evaluated using the October 2026 snapshot. Model selection used target months through December 2023; its decision was recorded in October 2026 before the retained test results were generated. Historical public-data vintages were not reconstructed.
  • The experiment history. Model selection stayed fixed. A calibration-history defect was corrected after the first test run; point forecasts were unchanged, and the corrected interval results are the ones reported.
  • What was fixed in advance. The registered model form, the promotion rule and the review thresholds were committed before the first forecast row existed. The data snapshot was retrieved in the same session, so the rule was registered alongside the data rather than before it.
  • The locked test for DSQ. At one month ahead, the candidate's 80% range held 22 of 24 months, and its average miss was 246 reports a month against the trailing mean's 265.
  • Did the ranges hold? At one month ahead, across the seven codes, they held between 33% and 100% of locked-test months: two inside the band written before the test, two above it, and three below the 70% floor, where the review rule is switched off.
  • The review rule. A month is flagged when the observed count exceeds the 80% upper bound and is at least five reports above the point forecast. Two consecutive flagged months open an episode. As a retrospective, filtered demonstration it opened one episode where it is on, and would open seven if it ran in every code; each code's rule was switched on from its coverage in the 2024-01 to 2025-12 locked test, the first 24 of the 32 months it is then applied to.
  • Recall context. Of the 24 Class I recall initiations in the evaluated span, thirteen in codes whose rule is off, 0 were preceded by the start of a queue episode within the eighteen months before; the rule that flags every month would have preceded 23. Association only: no recall-prediction result is claimed.

03 / Decisions and trade-offs

The choices that shaped the result, and what each one cost

Claude Code implemented the approved plan, and AI agents ran the simulated chart reviews. Each choice below is recorded with its counterfactual in the decision record.

  • Counting reports, not events or rates. The target is distinct reports per code per receipt month, the one figure the source can certify; it says nothing about events, which summary reports and missing event dates blur, or about rates, which need a denominator the source does not carry (D1).
  • A fixed candidate and a promotion bar. One ETS form was fixed before any test and replaced the better of two simple baselines only where its development-period error was at least 10% lower without a worse interval score; it earned use on two of seven codes, and the trailing three-month mean runs the rest (D6, D8).
  • Ranges from the model's own errors. Each 80% range comes from the 36 latest errors of the same model and horizon, not from the model's assumptions; it is honest about past misses and slow to follow a series that changes level, which is how three codes' ranges fell short (D7, D16).
  • Keeping the band where it was registered. When five of seven codes landed outside the 60% to 95% band written before the test, neither the band nor the ranges were moved; the three codes below 70% stay on the page with their review rule off (D16).
  • A two-month trigger with a five-report floor. A month is flagged only above its 80% range and at least five reports over the point, and an episode needs two flagged months in a row, so a single receipt pile-up does not open one; the cost is a flag that arrives a month later (D9).
  • Leading with three months ahead. The page leads with November 2026 at three months ahead because September 2026, one month ahead, had already ended when the forecast was issued; promotion was judged at one month ahead, and at three months ranges like November's held 20 of 22 locked-test months (D10, D19).
  • A private exclusion with a public receipt. Reports whose manufacturer or brand fields match a private list are removed whole, 1 of 649,080 here, and the count and the list's hash are published; a reader can check the receipt but not the list (D3).

04 / How the evidence was checked

What was checked, and what was not

  • Two paths. A separately written path, DuckDB SQL over the staged FDA pages plus its own Python arithmetic, recomputes the monthly and lag-matched counts, the distinct report totals, every forecast point and range, the scores, the review episodes, and the queue with and without its coverage gate as this page publishes it, and they must agree before anything is published. It does not refit the ETS model: candidate points are recomputed from the model's exported states, and ranges and scores from the published points. The recall timeline is checked only against fourteen independently verified Class I events, and the cohort gate, the exclusion receipt, the reports without an event date, the promotion decision, the pre-registered recall count, the summary-report composition, the remedial-recall counts and the outlook's twenty dots are carried from the build without a second derivation.
  • Checks that can fail. The domain gate runs 21 checks and re-runs them against deliberately corrupted copies of their input in 39 scenarios; the validation report records that every one tripped. Golden fixtures, computed outside both engines and committed before either existed, pass on both paths.
  • The chart review. Four AI agents reviewed rendered charts using simulated reviewer personas. They were instructed to use only the supplied images; reviewer isolation was not independently verified. Their findings exposed a concrete ambiguity: disabled review lanes still appeared to contain active episodes.

05 / The next test

What a quality manager does with a flag, and what is tested next

The intended workflow, for a quality manager whose code is flagged:

  1. Open the underlying reports for the flagged months and read their reporting context: the report types, the filers, and any batch or summary filing.
  2. Record a review disposition, including when nothing is found.
  3. Do not read an episode as a device-safety conclusion. It is a departure from expected reporting volume, and nothing more.

The live edge. A live-edge pipeline is implemented and passed a manual run. Weekly runs begin at publication. It stores source vintages and is designed to score a forecast when the target month first appears in the source. Live months are counted on a different basis from the frozen ones: raw count by receipt date as seen at the vintage, with no exclusion applied.

What is tested next. Two separate steps. The shuffled-dates reference tests whether the observed timing of episodes before Class I recall initiations differs from a chance reference, the same count recomputed with the dates shuffled; until it runs, the pre-registered recall count means association only. Prospective scoring is a separate next step: each frozen forecast is scored as its month first appears in the source, beginning with September 2026 and November 2026.
