The question Why it matters

A reporting system is good at looking back. This module asks the forward question of the same data: for each code, how many reports should FDA expect to receive next month, how wide is the honest range, did ranges like it hold on a test the model never saw, and which months, under a rule fixed in advance, would have earned a closer look. For @@headline@@ (@@headline_name@@) the answer for @@o_target@@ is about @@o_point@@ reports, with an 80% range of @@o_lo80@@ to @@o_hi80@@. The ranges held on the locked test for some codes and not others, and the page says which, because that is the part a reader has to know before trusting any of it.

01 / The decision and the outlook

What volume to expect next, and how sure to be

The decision this page serves is a quality team's monthly choice of where to look first: which product codes are reporting as expected and which are not. Whose decision: the owner of a post-market review queue. Horizon: one month, with three months as context. Literacy: a reader who works in spreadsheets and reads a trend line, not a statistician. Benchmark: the trailing three-month mean, the figure a spreadsheet would already give. Cadence: monthly as the source loads. Action: open a review or do not.

Receipts run through @@as_of@@ and the source had loaded them as of @@last_updated@@; this page was built from a snapshot taken @@retrieved@@. So the @@o_target@@ figure is an elapsed-period estimate: the month ended before the figure was issued and the source had not loaded it. It is labelled that way on the chart and it is never scored as a forecast.

Live edge. @@live_edge@@

02 / The test

Whether the forecasts earned trust on months the model never saw

Model selection used targets from 2022-01 to 2023-12 and was frozen at 2023-12 in the repository (config/model.json, commit @@model_commit@@) before any locked-period forecast existed. The locked test is 24 months, 2024-01 to 2025-12, from origins at or after 2023-12, and it ran once. The recent check, 2026-01 to 2026-08, is reported beside it and was not used for anything.

For @@headline@@ the model in use missed by @@c2_mae_use@@ reports a month on average over the locked test (candidate @@c2_mae_c@@, trailing mean @@c2_mae_a@@), and its 80% range covered @@c2_cov@@ of those months. The largest miss, chosen as the largest absolute error and nothing else, was @@c2_miss_month@@: @@c2_miss_actual@@ reports arrived against @@c2_miss_point@@ expected.

@@c2_card@@

Did the ranges hold? An 80% range should contain about four months in five. Across the seven codes the model in use covered between @@c3_lo@@ and @@c3_hi@@ of locked-test months, and @@c3_disabled@@ code(s) fell below the 70% floor the review rule needs, so their rule is switched off and the queue says so. Ranges too wide and ranges too narrow are both failures of calibration, and both are shown. A code with the rule off is not reviewed by this module, and nothing here stands in for it.

03 / What deserves review

The rule, every episode, and the workload it implies

The rule, fixed before the data was pulled (governance/pre-registration.md, commit @@prereg_commit@@): a month is flagged when its actual exceeds the model in use's 80% upper range by at least five reports; an episode is a run of two or more consecutive flagged months; one run is one episode; an empty queue is a valid result. Under a calibrated model about one episode in a hundred evaluated months would open by chance.

Over @@c4_months@@ evaluated code-months the rule flagged @@c4_flagged@@ months and opened @@c4_eps@@ episode(s), @@c4_rate@@ per evaluated month. The queue orders attention; a person decides what is in it. A flag is a departure from expected reporting volume and nothing more: it does not say why, and it is not a statement about any device or firm.

04 / What was checked

Every number on this page was re-derived down a second path

  • The freeze. @@total_raw@@ reports across the seven codes, pulled from openFDA in 696 pages whose SHA-256 is recorded in the manifest and listed in the source register. The monthly counts reconcile with zero variance, on every one of 896 code-months, against the source's own daily count series pulled separately.
  • The exclusion. A private token list, kept outside the repository, removed @@excluded@@ report(s) whole; its SHA-256 (@@sha_local@@) and the counts removed per field are published, and no firm is named anywhere.
  • Two paths. Every published cell, the counts, the lag-matched counts, the forecast points from exported model states, the ranges, the scores and the review episodes, is recomputed by a separately written SQL path (validate_measures.py) and must agree before anything is published.
  • Golden fixtures, written by hand before any engine code: both baselines on a made series, the ETS recurrence from fixed states, the review rule with a reset, the lag-matched count and the interval scores, passing on both paths.
  • Proof the checks can fail. The validation report feeds each check a deliberately corrupted copy of its input, a missing partition, a duplicate key, a report dropped from one of two codes, a blanked date, a future observation, an alias the list does not own, a count perturbed by one, a forecast whose target equals its origin, and records that each one tripped.
  • Chronology. The git log shows the pre-registration and the frozen harness before the first forecast row, the promotion decision before the first locked row, and one locked run.
  • The chart review and the reading panel under VIZ-PRINCIPLES v2.8 and CHART-REVIEW v2.8, recorded in the chart review.

05 / Limits and transfer

What these counts can and cannot say

@@disclaimer@@

FDA's own words, from the openFDA documentation: MDR data alone cannot be used to establish rates of events; a causal relationship cannot be established between product and the reactions listed in a report; and every API response says to assume all results are unvalidated. Reporting is passive, mixed voluntary and mandatory, batched by filers, moved by litigation and media and by a recall itself, and never carries a denominator. A quiet code is not a safe code.

What is counted. One report is one record the source holds, in the month FDA received it. These seven codes are ineligible for malfunction summary reporting, so one record stays one report across the whole window; that is why they were chosen. @@c5_missing@@ of the headline code's eligible reports carry no event date and are counted beside the lag chart, not in it.

@@c5_card@@

What a real new-product quality model needs that public data cannot supply: exposure (units in use, by lot and by site), design-verification and validation results, the unresolved-defect backlog, supplier and process measures, complaint intake before it becomes a report, and launch milestones. This module forecasts the one public proxy honestly and stops there.

Three things this page refuses to do. It names no manufacturer or brand, by decision, and the exclusion that keeps it at arm's length from any one firm is applied by a private list with a public receipt. It publishes no rate and no risk. And it does not read the recall timeline as a validation of the rule: the shuffled-dates test that could make it one is recorded as the next build, not claimed here.
